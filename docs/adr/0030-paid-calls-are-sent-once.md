# ADR-0030: A paid call is sent once, and a lost answer is recorded

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Issue: #266
- Builds on: the job queue's retry policy and the cost records of the MVP spec, ADR-0028 (never paying twice for a clip on the plan)
- Amended by: ADR-0031 (Claude's answers are streamed: its adapter applies the same rules itself instead of `send_paid`, read after the status and before the answer, over `UreqTransport::for_streams`; Claude's own timeout and a failure it asks not to be sent again are lost answers; a refusal carries its usage only when Claude bills it)

## Context

Every adapter reaches its provider through the `Transport` seam, and its only failure was a message. Adapters mapped any transport failure to `Unreachable`, the job queue treats `Unreachable` and `ProviderDown` as passing (`JobFailureKind::ProviderUnavailable`) and runs the job again, four attempts in all, and `Backoff` resent a request on any 429, 500, 502, 503, 504 or 529.

That is right for a free call and for a request that never left the computer. It is wrong for a paid, synchronous call (text, an image, speech, music, speech-to-text, a voice change, a timing, a typed decision) whose request was sent: a timeout while the provider is still working, a connection dropped while the answer comes back, or a gateway's 502 or 504 in front of a provider that finished, all leave the provider having done and billed the work. Sending it again pays again, up to four times per click, and none of it reached Costs, so budgets and a video's spend cap undercounted exactly when something was going wrong.

A second gap is next to it: a call that was answered but gave nothing usable (an answer cut at the token limit, a refusal, an image answer without an image) was billed too, and was recorded nowhere, because only a successful result carried its usage.

`ureq` cannot say on its own whether a request left: with one overall timeout, every expiry is reported as the same error whatever the phase, and a failed TLS handshake surfaces as a plain I/O error.

Clips and takes are asynchronous (submit, then poll) and have their own handling: an idempotency key or a saved request id, and ADR-0028 for the plan. They are out of this decision.

## Decision

1. **The transport says whether the request left.** `TransportError` carries a `Delivery`: `NotSent` (the provider cannot have acted) or `Unknown` (the request was, or may have been, received). For a request with a body, the `ureq` transport hands the body over through a reader that knows what is left of it, with an explicit `content-length` so the wire format is unchanged. The length is announced, so a server cannot act on a request while part of its body is still on this computer: an error before the whole body was handed over is `NotSent` (no connection, no TLS session, a large upload cut on its way), any error after it is `Unknown`. A request without a body is told by the error (name resolution, a refused connection, proxy, TLS setup and URL errors are `NotSent`; the rest `Unknown`). A successful status whose body breaks off before its end is an error (`Unknown`) rather than an empty answer; a failed status keeps its status even when its body cannot be read.
2. **A lost answer is its own failure.** `ProviderFailureKind::AnswerLost` and `JobFailureKind::AnswerLost` (`answer_lost`) mean the request was sent and no usable answer came back. It is not transient: the job fails after that one request, its text says the provider may have done and charged for the work, and the user decides when to retry. `Unreachable` now means the request never left (or the call was free) and stays transient, so the queue still retries with the network off.
3. **Paid calls resend only on answers that started nothing.** Paid adapters send through `Backoff::send_paid`, each with a test for its provider's own error report (Anthropic's `{"type": "error", ...}`, Google's `{"error": {"status": ...}}`, the `{"detail": ...}` of ElevenLabs and JEV). It resends on a 429, and on a 503 or 529 that is the provider's own report ("overloaded", "busy"): the provider answered that it did not start. A 500 in the provider's own report goes back to the adapter as before (`ProviderDown`, which the queue retries): the provider says the request failed, which it does not bill. Any other 5xx (a 502 or 504, or a 500, 503 or 529 that is a page, empty, or some other service's JSON) comes from something in front of the provider, says nothing about the work behind it, and is `AnswerLost`. So is a successful status whose body cannot be read (too long, not text, not JSON, or an empty file): the work was done and what came of it is gone. A transport error is `Unreachable` when the request never left and `AnswerLost` otherwise. Free calls (key checks, listings, status polls, downloads) keep `Backoff::send` and its wider retries.
4. **A new connection per paid call.** Paid adapters use `UreqTransport::for_paid_calls`, which keeps no idle connections. A kept connection that died unnoticed would take the request before failing, and a request that never arrived would look like one that may have.
5. **A failure can carry what it cost.** `ProviderFailure::billed` holds the model and usage of a call that was answered but gave no result: Claude's usage on a refusal, an answer cut at `max_tokens` or without text; Gemini's on an answer without a usable image; the characters ElevenLabs billed for speech that cannot be used; JEV's on answers that cannot be used.
6. **Costs records both.** `CostBook::record_failed`, called where each paid job handles its failure, records the billed usage when the failure carries it (and it is not all zeros), priced from the rate table like any call. For `AnswerLost` it records what such a call typically uses: the quantities known before the call (prompt size, characters, seconds of audio) and, for the rest, the mean of recent calls for the same purpose or the first guess the estimates already use. Either way it is a cost record like any other, on the job, the video and the channel, so budgets and spend caps count it. Any other failure started nothing and records nothing. No schema change: the record is priced from the rate table, as most records are.
7. **Scope.** Applied to Claude (text), Gemini (images), ElevenLabs (speech, music, speech-to-text, voice changer, isolator, forced alignment) and JEV (decisions). Out of it:
   - Clip and take adapters keep their own submission rules (a saved request id, a note of what was sent; #263 for the two that lack one).
   - A paid call in flight when the app closes or crashes never returns a failure to decide on; the job resumes and sends it again (#289).
   - A post on X is a paid synchronous call on the publishing side and still retries after a lost answer (#290).

## Consequences

- While Bardo stays open, one click makes at most one paid request whose fate is unknown. A provider outage that answers in the provider's own words is still retried by the queue; a gateway failure in front of it is not, and the user retries by hand. That trades some convenience during gateway incidents for never paying several times for one result.
- The estimate of a lost answer can be wrong in both directions: the provider may not have billed at all, or billed more than a typical call. It errs toward counting, because an undercount hides spending from budgets; the guide tells the user to compare with the provider's usage page when the amount matters.
- A lost answer's record cannot be told from a real charge afterwards: it has no mark of its own, Costs shows totals, and it cannot be taken back when the provider turns out not to have charged. It also counts among the recent calls later estimates are averaged from, which changes little since it was itself built from them. Marking such records would need a schema change, left for when it is needed.
- "Handed over" is a safe upper bound, not proof of arrival: the last piece of a body given to the connection may not have been written, so a few requests that never arrived are reported as lost answers and estimated in Costs. The error never goes the other way.
- An answered call whose JSON is valid but lacks what Bardo needs (a transcript without words, a timing without characters) fails as unexpected and, where the answer states no usage, records nothing.
- A retry after a lost answer is a new paid request. Where the provider offers an idempotency key, using it would let a retry collect the first result instead. Streaming does not: Claude's answers are streamed since ADR-0031, which keeps an answer from being cut by a clock, and one that stops midway is still lost.
- Every new paid adapter must choose `send_paid` (or, when it reads its answer as a stream, apply the same rules as Claude's does), name its provider's error report and attach billed usage to its failures, and every new paid job must call `record_failed`.
