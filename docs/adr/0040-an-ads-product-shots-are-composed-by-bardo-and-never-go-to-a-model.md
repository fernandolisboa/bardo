# ADR-0040: An ad's product shots are composed by Bardo and never go to a model

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Issue: #278
- Builds on: the storyboard scene plan (#258), product images (#274), ad projects and the ad brief (ADR-0038), the ad script (ADR-0039), paid calls sent once (ADR-0030), the plan sized from its narration (ADR-0031)

## Context

An ad has to show the product. Until now every scene of every plan was a picture the image model draws from a prompt, so the only way to show a product was to have a model draw something like it: the wrong label, a made-up screen, a logo that is almost right. The user's own images of the product, imported with a confirmation that they may be used (#274), were not used by any scene.

Sending those images to an image or video model to "place" the product is the other obvious route. It costs money per scene, redraws the product (which a screenshot must never be), and hands the user's product pictures to a provider for every ad.

## Decision

1. **A scene of an ad has a kind.** `SceneKind` is *drawn*, *product in use*, *product shot* or *end card*, stored on the scene (migration 0072; rows from before read as drawn). Scenes of every other project are drawn scenes and are parsed, drawn and estimated exactly as before: their plan's request, schema and payload do not change.
2. **The product's images decide which kinds exist.** `ProductPictures::allows`: a product shot needs an image, the product in use a photo (a screen is only ever shown as it is), and a drawn scene and the end card are always allowed. The planner's JSON schema only lists the allowed kinds, the prompt says which they are, and the inspector offers only those, with the end card on the last scene.
3. **An ad's plan comes from its own template, and the product facts do not depend on its text.** *Ad scene plan* is the storyboard plan with one scene per sentence, the hook first, the end card last, and the product on screen by the time the narration names it. The request lists the product's images by position, kind and shape (never their content) and the allowed kinds; a template edited to leave either out still sends both, after the prompt. The ad rules of ADR-0038 and the fixed rules of an ad's scenes follow the instructions. The job's payload carries the image list the request numbered, so the answer is read against the same numbering even if the product's images changed meanwhile.
4. **An answer is made to fit, not refused.** A kind Bardo does not know or the product does not allow is a drawn scene; an end card that is not the last scene is a product shot; a composed scene naming an image the product does not have shows the first. A plan is never lost, and paid for again, over a kind.
5. **A product shot is composed by Bardo, from pixels, on the computer.** The `ShotComposer` port (domain) is implemented in `bardo-media` in plain Rust, without the ffmpeg sidecar: decode (PNG, JPEG, WebP, with the photo's orientation), scale with Lanczos to fit, never stretch or cut, round the card's corners, and set it over a plain backdrop in a tone of the image's average color. No model sees the image, no key is needed and nothing is paid. In this slice the end card of a product with an image is a product shot, and the product in use is drawn like any drawn scene; the product reference and the end card's own layout come with later slices.
6. **Where the card goes is a domain rule.** `ShotLayout` places it inside what the strongest zoom still shows, less a margin, and above the caption band, the lower 38% of the screen (`CAPTION_BAND`), from the zoom's first moment to its last. A test in `bardo-media` holds every caption style to that band for the captions Bardo makes (two lines in a vertical frame, one in a wide one). Because a pan would still carry the card out of the frame, **a composed scene only zooms and is always shown whole**: the one place a cut is built from a plan turns a pan on a composed scene into a zoom in of the same strength and fits its picture to the frame, and the editor does the same to a pan or a window asked for one. In a frame of the picture's own shape, fitting fills it exactly; in a render of the other shape (a long ad posted to a 9:16 network, or the other way round) the picture shows whole with bars instead of being cut. An image shown more than 10% larger than its own pixels is reported as too small for the frame.
7. **Composing runs in jobs, never on the UI thread.** The plan job composes the plan's product shots right after saving it (a failure there is logged and leaves the scene waiting, it does not fail a plan that was paid for). The image job composes the ones still missing before it draws, and a run with only shots to compose asks for no key, no budget and no consent. Changing a scene to a composed kind starts such a job at once.
8. **A composed scene keeps what it had.** Its drawn image and clip stay on the scene, unused, so changing back to a drawn kind is free; when the scene never had a drawing, changing back draws nothing: a drawing is paid, so the user asks for it and sees its estimate first. `Scene::picture` is what a scene shows: the composed picture of the image a composed scene names (none while a picture of another image waits to be replaced), else its drawn image. A change of kind clears the scene's failure, which was about the picture it showed before.
9. **A composed picture never goes to a model, in any direction.** `Scene::can_animate` is false for a composed scene, so no clip job, in any production mode, takes one, and a clip job tried again leaves a scene that became composed meanwhile. A composed scene is no place, previous-shot or look reference for another scene, is not listed as the set of a performed take, is not a thumbnail background, and is skipped when scenes are drawn again for a new style or new portraits.
10. **Composed scenes are free in every estimate.** The whole-video estimate counts no image and no clip for them. Before an ad has a plan it is estimated with a scene every 3 seconds, of which two product shots and the end card are free when the product has an image, and its plan is sized a scene a sentence instead of a scene every 6 seconds.
11. **A composed picture records what it was made from.** The product image and the frame's shape are stored with the file. A scene whose picture is of another shape (the video's format changed), or that has none, waits to be composed again and reads *To compose*; composing again is free, and **Compose product shots** does it without drawing anything, so it never waits on a key. When the product image is gone, the scene fails saying so and keeps the picture it had, until the user picks another image or kind.

## Consequences

- The product in an ad's product shot is the user's own picture, pixel for pixel apart from scaling: nothing is invented, and a screenshot is never redrawn.
- An ad with product images costs less than a video of the same length: its composed scenes are neither drawn nor animated.
- `bardo-media` gains the `image` crate (PNG, JPEG and WebP decoding, PNG encoding), built with optimizations in the dev profile so composing stays quick in tests.
- A product shot never moves like a clip: it is a still with a slow zoom. Layouts other than the card, other backdrops and a product cut out of its background are later slices.
- Captions of more lines than Bardo makes (text the user typed into one caption) can still reach above the band; the band is sized for the captions Bardo makes.
- The "too small" warning, and the shape the planner is told for each image, are worked out from the image's size as stored; a photo whose file says it is rotated is composed upright, so for such a photo both may be a little off.
- A render in another shape than the video's shows a composed picture whole with bars, smaller than it would be composed for that shape; composing a picture for each render shape comes with the placement exports.
- Nothing forces the last scene of an ad's plan to be the end card, or a product shot to exist: the template asks for both and an answer without them is kept as it is. The user can change any scene's kind.
- Removing a product image does not remove the pictures already composed from it: an ad keeps the product shot it shows until the scene is composed again.
