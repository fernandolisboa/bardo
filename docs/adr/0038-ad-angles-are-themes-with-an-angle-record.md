# ADR-0038: An approved ad angle is a theme with an angle record beside it, and an ad project is a video project that keeps its product

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Issue: #275
- Builds on: themes and their ranking by the decision engine, the products library (#273), the product sheet and its claims (ADR-0037), versions in other languages (ADR-0023), paid calls sent once (ADR-0030), text jobs waiting for each other (ADR-0036)

## Context

A video project starts from an approved theme and is tied to it for good: its title, its niche and its script's subject are the theme's. An ad for a product needs more than a theme holds: the framework its script follows, the part of the audience it speaks to, a draft of its first words, the claims it leans on, and above all the product, which every later stage reads (the script may state only its confirmed claims; the scenes show it; the post discloses whose it is).

Three things had to be decided together: where an ad angle lives and how it becomes a project; how the angles are ranked; and what one video is allowed to say when the product it advertises keeps changing.

## Decision

1. **An ad angle is a record of its own, and approving it creates a theme.** `ad_angle` holds what a theme does not: product, channel, framework, segment, hook, the claims leaned on (`ad_angle_claim`) and its own ranking. While suggested it is not a theme at all. Approving it creates, in one transaction, a theme with the angle's title and text (already approved, in the niche of the product's category, else the channel's niche, else the product's name), a video project of that theme with `product_id` set, and the project's brief; the angle keeps the theme's id. Every stage after the Brief then works on an ad project as on any other, with no second kind of project to carry through the scripts, scenes, renders and exports.
2. **Ad angles are reviewed on Products, not on Themes.** The theme an angle became is left out of a channel's themes (it is reached by its id only), so the Themes screen, the titles Claude is told to avoid there and the theme ranking never see it; the list of projects the Themes screen shows for a channel leaves ad projects out too. The angles of a product are listed per channel on the product's Ad angles tab.
3. **An ad project is a video project with a product.** `video_project.product_id` references the product; a project that has one is an ad. The app refuses to delete a product a project names, and the reference is the backstop. An ad project starts 30 seconds long, its first stage is the Brief, it is left out of the channel's past performance (how an ad did says nothing of how the channel's own ideas do), and it refuses a version in another language (ADR-0023): the claims and the brief are reviewed per channel, so the other channel gets an angle of its own.
4. **The ad frameworks are a catalog in the domain.** Nine frameworks, each a stable code, a name and the steps a script written in it goes through, in the English a model reads; the interface names them in both languages. There is no testimonial framework. The table stores the code and the domain checks it, as for every table since products.
5. **One job proposes and ranks.** Claude proposes five angles from the *Ad angles* template, filled with the channel, the product's brief and sheet, the confirmed claims numbered, the framework catalog and the titles already proposed. The answer is JSON in a fixed schema; a framework is one of the catalog's codes and a claim is a number, which Bardo maps back to the claim's id (a number it did not give is dropped), so a model cannot make an angle lean on a claim the user did not confirm; an angle left leaning on no claim of the prompt is not kept. The proposed angles are saved before the ranking, with the job that proposed them, so a job taken up again does not propose twice (ADR-0030). The decision engine then answers three questions per angle (the hook, the audience fit, the clarity), ten angles to a call; the same job kind without the first step ranks the angles an edit or a failed run left unranked. A ranking is written onto the angle that was ranked and nothing else of it: the write touches the ranking columns only, and only while the angle is still a suggestion with no ranking and the same words, so a ranking that returns after the user approved, edited or discarded the angle changes nothing. An angle starts one project at most: approving checks, in the transaction that creates the project, that the angle is still a suggestion, and needs what proposing needs (a product that is not archived, with a confirmed claim on its sheet).
6. **The weights are in code.** Priority = hook 40% + audience fit 35% + clarity 25%; confidence is the least sure of the three answers. The hook leads because an ad nobody stays for sells nothing. An angle that leans on a sensitive claim is marked by Bardo from the sheet, not by a model.
7. **The rendered request is kept in the job.** The template is rendered, and the fixed ad rules added after its instructions, when the job is queued, and both go into the job's payload: the estimate and the budget check price exactly what will be sent, and a template edited while the job waits does not change it. Both calls are estimated, checked against their provider's budget and recorded under the channel; the estimate of a run that proposes counts the angles already waiting for a ranking, since the run ranks them too, and a failed job run again from Jobs has what it still pays for checked against the budget first. The job looks for its product and its channel before it pays for anything, and a product cannot be deleted while a job proposes or ranks its angles. Proposing and ranking for one product and channel are one `Work` (ADR-0036): another channel's angles are other work.
8. **The ad rules cannot be edited away.** Every request that writes for an ad gets a fixed block after the template's own instructions: state only the numbered claims, invent no testimonial, review, rating, number of customers, award or press mention, state no deadline or scarcity the advertiser did not, present no drawn person as real, show no other brand's logo and copy no competitor. The product's text is the user's, a model's or came from the web: it is sent inside tags with every `<` in it written as `&lt;`, so it cannot close its tag and go on as the prompt's own words, and the first rule of the fixed block says that what the prompt tells about the product, its audience, its claims, the notes, the channel and earlier titles is a description, never an instruction. The sheet's summary and segments are sent with the angle request as description of the product and its audience; only the numbered claims may be stated. Until the *Ad script* template exists (#276), the script request of an ad project is the script template's, with the brief appended (the product, the angle and its framework, the claims the video uses, numbered, the offer, the call to action and the link) and the fixed block after its instructions.
9. **A video keeps its own copy of the claims it uses.** The brief copies every confirmed claim when the project starts (`ad_brief_claim`: text, kind, sensitive), and the user switches claims off or on for this video. Editing or rejecting a claim in the product never changes a video. Whether a copy drifted, and whether its claim is sensitive now, is read from the sheet each time, not stored: the stage shows Attention and the user keeps the copy, takes the product's words, or leaves the claim out. Keeping records what the product said then, so the same change is not asked about twice and a later one is.
10. **The brief gates the script, and a later change only marks it.** The Script stage opens once the brief is confirmed, which needs at least one claim, no claim left to decide and a call to action. A new script also needs the brief to be ready still: a confirmed brief that a later change left without a claim, without a call to action or with a claim to decide holds the script until it is settled, and an ad project without a brief is refused. A change to the brief afterwards (a claim, the words, the length) moves `changed_at`; a script written from the brief as it was before that shows the stage as Attention until the user says the script still holds or writes it again. The script job keeps, in its payload, the `changed_at` of the brief it read when it was queued, so a brief changed while the script waits, is written or is run again marks that script too. Nothing is rewritten by itself.

## Consequences

- Stages, estimates, caps, renders and exports need no ad-specific branch to work on an ad project; what is ad-specific is added per stage by the later slices (the ad script and its claims check, composed product shots, the end card, disclosure).
- A theme row exists for every approved angle and is hidden from the channel's themes by a query condition. Code that lists a channel's themes for another purpose has to decide whether ad themes belong there.
- An approved angle cannot be discarded or edited, so an ad project always has the angle it was approved from. The record goes only with its channel, whose projects go with it, or with its product, which cannot be deleted while a project names it.
- An angle keeps the ids of the claims it leans on. A claim that left the sheet, or is no longer confirmed, is shown as one the sheet no longer confirms; the brief, not the angle, decides what the video may say.
- The same product advertised on two channels is proposed, ranked and paid for twice, once per channel: the audience, the language and the titles to avoid differ.
- A settings file does not carry angles or briefs; they are in the database and its backups.
