# ADR-0037: Research on the web runs at the provider, behind a port, and what it writes waits for the user

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Issue: #274
- Builds on: the products library (#273), paid calls sent once (ADR-0030), streamed answers (ADR-0031), a cancelled job giving up its request to Claude (ADR-0034), the rate table and budgets of Costs

## Context

An ad needs more than the brief the user typed: who buys the product, what holds them back, what the alternatives are and what can honestly be said about it. Most of that is public, on the product's own page and around it. Reading it by hand is slow; reading it with a model means text from pages nobody in Bardo chose ends up in front of a model, and then in an ad that states things about a product in public.

Three questions had to be settled together: who fetches the pages, how far a run may go and what it may cost, and how far anything the run wrote is trusted.

## Decision

1. **A domain port, the web researcher.** `WebResearcher::research(key, request)` takes instructions, a prompt, pictures and `ResearchLimits` (searches, pages fetched, tokens read from any one page) and returns `ResearchNotes`: the text the model wrote, the pages it read (address, title, when) and everything the run used. `research_until` takes the job's `Stop`, as the text generator does (ADR-0034): a cancelled job sends no further request of the research and gives up the answer on its way. The app runs no browser and no scraper, and no other crate knows the provider's protocol.
2. **Claude implements it with Anthropic's server-side web search and web fetch tools.** The request names both tools with their limits (`max_uses`, `max_content_tokens`) and `allowed_callers: ["direct"]`, so Claude calls them itself: the newer default, running them from code Claude writes, adds a code-execution tool the limits do not cover, and uses made from that code are not counted against `max_uses` from one request of a turn to the next. A tool the research has no use of is not offered. The link to read first is written in the prompt, since the provider fetches only addresses the request or an earlier result named.
3. **The limits hold over the whole research.** A product research makes at most 8 searches, fetches at most 5 pages and reads at most 20,000 tokens of any one. The API can pause a long turn (`pause_turn`); Bardo takes it up once by sending the paused answer back unchanged, after the same task and with the same tools, to the letter. The API counts the direct uses of a turn across its requests, so the continuation needs no smaller limit, and a thought the paused answer holds is valid only under the tools it was written with: a continuation that named other limits could be refused. A turn that pauses twice is given up. The room to write is shared between the two requests. The page limit cuts text; a PDF is not cut by it, so the research is told to fetch web pages only.
4. **The research asks for the provider's prompt cache.** Every search or fetch starts another step in which the model is given everything before it again. With the cache those tokens are billed as cache reads, a twentieth of the input price. Cache writes and cache reads are meters of their own in `Metered` and in the rate table, beside a meter for searches, priced per thousand.
5. **The estimate before a research is its ceiling, and each adapter answers for its own.** `WebResearcher::ceiling(limits, sent)` returns the most a run within the limits can use: every search and fetch made, every page at its limit, the cache written in the first request and again in a continuation (which should read the cache, since it sends the same start; the ceiling does not count on it), everything read again at every step in the worst order, and all the room to write. The app prices that with the rate table, shows it before the run and checks it against the provider's budget. A planned call carries its ceiling beside its usual size (`PlannedCall::with_ceiling`); what a run usually costs is shown beside the ceiling once runs were recorded. A lost answer is still recorded at the usual size (ADR-0030), not at the ceiling.
6. **Two paid calls in one job, with a checkpoint between them.** Step 1 is the research; its notes and pages are kept in the job's checkpoint before anything else happens. Step 2 is a plain text call with no tools that turns the brief and the notes into the product sheet as JSON in a fixed schema; its answer is kept in the checkpoint only once it parses, so an answer that cannot be used is not kept and not paid for twice by a retry that would read it again. A job taken up again never runs a step whose result it holds. Each call holds the paid-call note of ADR-0030 while it is out, and the note carries the search and cache meters, so a research cut short is recorded with them. A call that failed after paying for part of its work (a research whose continuation met an outage has its first answer billed) is recorded as billed and is not made again by the queue, whatever stopped it: the job fails as a request that failed, and runs again only when the user retries it. Both calls are given the job's stop. *Write from my brief* is the same job without step 1.
7. **What the web says is data, at every step.**
   - The research call has only the search and fetch tools. Nothing a page says can change anything in Bardo.
   - The notes go into the second call as quoted material, under fixed instructions that say so; that call has no tools at all and can only answer in the schema.
   - Bardo, not the model, decides what a claim's source is worth: every claim taken from the answer starts *found*, whatever source the answer gives it, including one drawn from the brief's own words. A claim that names a page the research did not read is dropped, and so is one that names a page when there was no research. A page is called the product's own only when its address is the brief's link or under it; another page of the same shop is a web page like any other. A claim that names the brief is the model's reading of the brief, which nothing checks, and the sheet says so beside it. The facts the user typed in the brief are added by Bardo itself, as confirmed; they are never taken from the answer.
   - A claim that contains a digit is sensitive whatever the model said.
   - Addresses are shown as text and opened only on the user's click, and only when they are `http` or `https`.
8. **A run adds and never overwrites.** Taking a written sheet in fills only the parts that are empty, adds rows and claims the sheet does not hold, and leaves every claim it knows as it is: confirmed, rejected or waiting. A rejected claim stays on the sheet, which is what keeps a later research from bringing it back as new, and it takes none of the 80 places a sheet has for claims; a run that finds the sheet full says how many claims it left out. A restricted category the user chose, *None* included, stays whatever a later run finds. A fact the user types in the brief after a model's claim said the same makes that claim theirs: typed and confirmed. Rows and pages are matched by name and address only: one the user removed is not remembered, and a later run that writes it again adds it again. So *Research again* is safe for everything the user reviewed, and taking the same answer in twice changes nothing.
9. **The sheet is not edited while a job fills it.** Edits of the sheet's text and claims are refused while the product's job is queued or running, so the job's last write cannot undo them. Edits waiting in the form are saved before a run starts. A brief saved meanwhile leaves the sheet to the job, which reads the brief again before it writes the sheet and when it takes the brief's facts. A product is not deleted while its job runs, and a failed job is not run again from Jobs while another one fills the same sheet.
10. **Costs.** Two cost purposes, *Product research* and *Product sheet*, recorded under no channel and no video: a research is paid per run and reused by every video of the product.

## Consequences

- The research is only as good as the provider's tools; a second provider is another adapter of the same port, with its own ceiling.
- The ceiling is deliberately high: about US$ 2.30 for a product research at November 2026 prices. About US$ 1.50 of it is the cache written twice, which a run that is not paused, or whose continuation reads the cache, pays once; about US$ 0.40 is all the room to write, about US$ 0.30 is everything read again at every step, and US$ 0.08 is the searches. A real run is expected well under US$ 1. Both figures are a first guess until a real-key run is measured; the usual cost shown beside the ceiling corrects itself as runs are recorded.
- The sheet call after a research is estimated at its usual size, not at a ceiling: its input is bounded by the notes the research may write.
- The limits across a pause are the API's own count. A real-key run checks that a paused research stays within them.
- The ceiling covers web pages. A PDF the research fetched against its instructions would be read whole, up to the provider's own PDF limits, and could pass the ceiling; what the run used is recorded as billed either way.
- A model can still write a wrong claim with a real source. That is what the review is for: nothing a model wrote reaches a script before the user confirms it, and the source is one click away.
- A page can hold text aimed at a model. It can at worst bend the notes, and through them propose claims and rows the user then reads; it has no tool to call and nothing it writes is used unreviewed.
- Search results and fetched pages are read by the provider, so the product's link and the searches a research makes are known to it, like any prompt.
